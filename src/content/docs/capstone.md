LocoLabo hosts student teams through Curtin's Industry Capstone program. The lab brings a real problem, a working codebase, and the training scaffold. Students bring the analysis, the data work, and the evaluation.

Semester 2 2026 is the pilot: two briefs, one per track.

## The two briefs

**Local AI versus cloud AI: a total cost of ownership model.** A business team builds a model for when local inference beats a cloud API, and when it does not. The useful result is the crossover point, not a single headline number, and the model has to survive a sceptical finance committee.

**Building and benchmarking a domain-specialist AI model.** A technical team picks a professional domain, assembles a few hundred worked examples, and trains a small adapter on Qwen3-4B. Then they measure whether the specialist beats the base model on a sealed benchmark. A rigorous negative result is a valid outcome.

## What students get

- A scaffolded training notebook that runs on Colab's free tier.
- Benchmarking time on lab hardware, arranged with the client.
- A defined contribution path into LocoLLM.
- A client for scope and data questions, and an academic supervisor for assessment.

## Access to the briefs

The capstone workspace, both briefs, the gate sheet, and the supervision pack are shared with allocated teams and supervisors. Ask the client for a copy.

Finished adapters follow the public [LocoLLM contribution guide](https://github.com/michael-borck/loco-llm/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Contact

Michael Borck is the client for both briefs. Scope, data, and benchmark questions go to him. Unit, report, and assessment questions go to the allocated academic supervisor.
